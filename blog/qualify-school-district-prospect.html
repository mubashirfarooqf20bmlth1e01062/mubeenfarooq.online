ACCOUNT QUALIFICATION & K-12 BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT

How to Qualify a School District Prospect Before Outreach

A large district list can create activity without creating useful opportunities. Before outreach, qualify each account against your service capabilities, public evidence, decision structure, procurement route and realistic timing—while keeping unknowns visible instead of turning assumptions into sales claims.

By Research Ops Editorial Team · November 20, 2026 · 7–9 minute read

General business-development guidance only. District service needs, language-access practices, professional learning priorities, procurement and contracting requirements vary. Verify current requirements with each district, relevant state authorities and qualified professionals.

Start with provider-side fit

Write down what your organization can genuinely deliver: service type, geographic coverage, staffing or implementation capacity, language coverage, technology requirements, service hours, lead times and operational limits. Include required credentials or licenses only where applicable to your actual service model and the relevant jurisdiction.

Define disqualifiers as well as positive criteria. A district outside your delivery area, a service model you cannot support, or a timeline your team cannot meet may be a poor prospect even if the district is large. This prevents outreach from creating expectations your organization cannot fulfill.

Verify the district and contracting entity

Confirm that the target is the right public school district or local education agency. The NCES Common Core of Data is the federal database for public elementary and secondary schools and districts; its District Locator helps check district identity and identifiers. Cross-check the official district website and state education agency because names, structures and contact pages can change.

Do not confuse a school campus, charter school, charter management organization, regional service agency and local district. Determine which entity would issue the solicitation or sign the agreement. Record district name, state, NCES ID where useful, official website, main phone, source URL and verification date.

Look for relevant evidence—not generic signals

Review official strategic plans, school improvement plans, department pages, board agendas and minutes, budget documents, public reports, job postings and procurement notices. Search for evidence connected to the service you offer: a published program, a stated improvement priority, an open solicitation or a documented service model.

Classify each finding as a verified fact, a possible signal or an unknown. A job posting may indicate a recruitment effort, but does not prove the district will outsource. A strategic priority does not prove a budget exists. An old board item may no longer reflect current plans. Save the document date and exact source so another researcher can reproduce the conclusion.

Map the decision and influence roles

Identify likely program owners and the people who may evaluate, approve, purchase or administer the service. Depending on the offering, this could include special education, student services, related services, multilingual learner programs, curriculum, technology, human resources, finance, procurement or school leadership.

Separate operational interest from purchasing authority. A director may explain a program but not control vendor selection; procurement may manage the formal process without owning the service need. Use official staff directories and published procurement channels. Record role, department, public contact source and date checked; mark responsibilities as unconfirmed until verified.

Check procurement and timing before outreach

Read the district’s purchasing and vendor pages. Look for registration steps, solicitation portals, insurance and screening information, required forms, contract templates, quote thresholds, bid calendars and rules for communicating with staff during an open competition. These vary by district, state and service category, so never present a checklist as universal legal requirements.

Determine whether there is an active RFP, an existing awarded contract, a vendor pool, an approved cooperative purchasing route or no visible process. An absence of public notice is not proof that a district is open to unsolicited proposals. If an active solicitation restricts contact, follow its instructions and communicate only through the designated channel.

Use a transparent qualification scorecard

A simple scorecard can compare accounts consistently. Rate each dimension high, medium, low or unknown, and keep the evidence beside the rating:

  • Service fit: Can the provider deliver the stated scope?
  • Geographic fit: Is the district within a supported territory?
  • Evidence: Is there a current, relevant public signal?
  • Role clarity: Are likely program and purchasing roles mapped?
  • Procurement: Is the vendor route understood?
  • Timing: Is there a verified planning or solicitation window?
  • Capacity: Can the provider meet realistic implementation needs?
Do not add points for guessed budgets, inferred shortages or unverified relationships. Unknown is useful information because it shows what research or discovery must clarify.

Hypothetical example: qualify before contacting

A school-based OT provider is considering a district in a neighboring state. NCES and the district website confirm the entity. A public strategic plan mentions inclusive student support, but no OT vacancy or purchasing notice is found. The provider checks its state-specific service and credential requirements, maps the special education and procurement roles, and reviews the vendor page. It records service fit as medium, strategic alignment as a possible signal, procurement route as known, and current buying timing as unknown. The next step is a neutral inquiry through the published vendor channel—not a claim that the district needs outsourced OT services.

Turn qualification into a disciplined next action

Choose one next step for each account: research further, monitor a public calendar, verify a contact, ask a permitted discovery question, register as a vendor, respond to an open solicitation or disqualify the account. Set a review date for records that may change. Keep source links, last-checked dates and the reason for each status in your CRM.

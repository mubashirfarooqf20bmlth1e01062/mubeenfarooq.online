K–12 MARKET GUIDE

How Speech Therapy Providers Can Research District Markets

Companies offering speech-language pathology services to schools need more than a list of district names. They need to understand which districts fit their service model, who oversees related services and how the district purchases outside services.

This guide provides general business development research context. District procedures vary. Confirm current requirements directly with the relevant school district and qualified professional or legal advisers where appropriate.

A practical framework

Define the speech and language service model

Clarify whether the organization offers in-person school-based SLP services, telepractice, evaluations, SLPA support, bilingual services or a combination. Identify states where the company can operate and credentials, supervision and delivery arrangements it can support.

Research the district's special education structure

Review special education, exceptional student education, student services and related services pages. Look for SLP department contacts, service descriptions, program information, public staffing notices and board materials. District department names vary, so search related terms.

Identify the relevant buyer group

Potential roles include Special Education Director, Director of Related Services, SLP Coordinator, Student Services Director and procurement staff. Map the program leader who understands the need alongside the purchasing contact who can explain the district's process.

Use public signals carefully

Job postings, board agendas, budget documents, public RFPs and vendor notices provide context. They do not prove that a district has a current vendor opening. Record what the source actually says, when it was published and whether it remains current.

Qualify geographic and delivery fit

Confirm state eligibility, in-person or remote delivery suitability, licensing, supervision, credentialing, background checks and insurance requirements. A market opportunity is useful only if the provider can meet applicable requirements.

Put the research into action

Research Ops can help define the target market, organize public-source research, map district contacts and coordinate next steps around your company's actual capabilities.
